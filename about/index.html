About

ResilientSolutionsforCoastal,Urban,EstuarineandRiverineEnvironments

RESCUER is a Doctoral Network funded by the European Union's Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, training a new generation of researchers in the modeling and forecasting of water-related hazards.

Mission

Ten young researchers, one shared challenge

The RESCUER Doctoral Network trains ten young researchers to tackle the medium- and long-term water challenges facing coastal communities.

The focus is on forecasting and modeling coastal, riverine and urban flooding, and the water-quality problems that come with them. Each doctoral candidate works on their own research project, enrolled in a doctoral program at a European university, and is part of a network that spans eight beneficiaries and ten associated partners.

Vision

From numerical breakthroughs to safer coasts

The past decade has brought major advances in the numerical treatment of multi-scale and multi-physics problems. RESCUER turns these advances into models that meet the pressing needs of practical hydrodynamic modeling in the coastal and estuarine zone, with one goal: improving the overall safety of coastal areas.

Objectives

What RESCUER sets out to do

  1. 01

    Train ten doctoral researchers

    Give ten young researchers the scientific, computational and transferable skills to tackle medium- and long-term water challenges facing coastal communities.

  2. 02

    Turn numerical advances into practical models

    Bring a decade of progress in multi-scale and multi-physics numerical methods into models that engineers and forecasters can actually use.

  3. 03

    Forecast hazards and water quality

    Improve the modeling and forecasting of coastal, riverine and urban flooding, and of the water-quality problems that come with them.

  4. 04

    Connect academia and practice

    Link universities, research institutes, companies and public authorities through joint supervision, secondments and pilot sites.

Research themes

Four work packages, one water system

The research follows water from the open coast and the river to the city and back to the sea.

All research
WP1

Coastal hazards

Wave-by-wave models of the nearshore zone for operational forecasting of run-up, overtopping and coastal flooding.

WP2

River & estuary dynamics

Fast, accurate models of flows over erodible beds, coupled with groundwater and sediment transport.

WP3

Urban flooding

High-resolution flood models for cities, including buildings, street furniture, cars and floating debris, validated in the laboratory.

WP4

Water quality

Tracking pollutants, bacteria, sediments and plastics from cities and rivers to estuaries and the surf zone.

The programme

What is an MSCA Doctoral Network?

Doctoral Networks are part of the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA), the European Union's flagship programme for researchers' training and careers under Horizon Europe.

A Doctoral Network funds a joint doctoral training programme run by a partnership of universities, research institutions and non-academic organizations such as companies and public bodies. The doctoral candidates are employed by their host organization, follow a doctoral program leading to a PhD, and spend secondments at other partners, including outside academia.

Alongside their research, they receive network-wide training in scientific and transferable skills, from numerical methods and high-performance computing to open science, communication and career development.
