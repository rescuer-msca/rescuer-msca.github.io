Research theme · WP1

Coastal hazards

Wave-by-wave models of the nearshore zone for operational forecasting of run-up, overtopping and coastal flooding.

The challenge

Storm waves, swell and wind drive most of the damage on exposed coasts. Close to the shore, waves interact with steep and irregular sea beds, submarine canyons, breakwaters and fjords. Phase-averaged models, the workhorse of operational forecasting, cannot always capture these effects. Engineers and coastal managers need tools that resolve each wave, and that run fast enough to be used every day.

Our approach

RESCUER develops phase-resolving numerical tools that represent the dynamics of individual waves in the nearshore zone, with a focus on Boussinesq-type and Green–Naghdi models. Our fellows work on:

  • coupling wave models with a meso-scale atmospheric model so that wind forcing acts directly on the free surface;
  • stable, memory-efficient simulation methods on structured multi-block and curvilinear grids for narrow channels and complex coastlines;
  • operational wave forecasting over highly irregular bathymetry, such as the Capbreton submarine canyon in southwest France, using GPU-accelerated codes;
  • tracing ocean swell into Norwegian fjord systems, in connection with the planned floating bridge over Bjørnafjorden.

Why it matters

Better nearshore forecasts mean better early warnings, safer coastal infrastructure and more reliable design of structures such as bridges and breakwaters.
