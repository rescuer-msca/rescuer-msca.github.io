Study sites

Our models are developed for real places. These are the coasts, estuaries, fjords and cities where RESCUER fellows compare simulations with field measurements.

Where we work

WP1 · Coastal hazards

Anglet urban waterfront

France

Second Fellow 3 case study, where man-made bathymetric gradients affect wave overtopping on urban waterfronts and wave-induced overflow up tidal channels. Anglet also hosts the UPPA SIAME laboratory.

Fellows: Panu Pietikäinen

WP1 · Coastal hazards

Bjørnafjorden

Norway

Fjord south of Bergen where the Norwegian Public Roads Administration (Statens vegvesen, an associated partner) plans a partially floating bridge for the E39 coastal highway. Buoys have recorded swell that phase-averaged models such as SWAN reproduce poorly. The Fellow 4 project targets swell propagation in fjords with a stable Boussinesq model over a 30 km x 30 km domain.

Fellows: Yago Pego Martínez

WP1 · Coastal hazards

Capbreton submarine canyon and Hossegor–Labenne coast (South Aquitaine)

France

Coastal segment shaped by the Capbreton submarine canyon, a natural extreme bathymetric gradient. Fellow 3 studies how the canyon modifies the wave field and the resulting wave run-up, overtopping and flooding during storms. Work so far includes ray tracing over the South Aquitaine coast, with wave focusing seen off Hossegor and Labenne. Results are intended to feed SUEZ-RPT early-warning systems.

Fellows: Panu Pietikäinen

WP4 · Water quality

Ebro River at Zaragoza (Zaragoza–Gelsa reach)

Spain

Real-world application of a coupled shallow-water and reactive-transport model for emerging contaminants (sulfamethoxazole). It simulates the December 2021 Ebro flood with a hypothetical hospital-effluent release on the Huerva tributary, using Zaragoza and Huerva gauging-station data.

Fellows: Mengyun Wang

WP4 · Water quality

French Basque coast

France

The Fellow 10 project plans to reproduce historical bacterial pollution events recorded along the Basque coast of SW France. It extends a Boussinesq-type model (BOSZ) to dissolved and floating pollutants, with drifter experiments for validation.

Fellows: Víctor Loras Herrero

WP2 · River & estuary dynamics

Guadalquivir estuary

Spain

Large estuary in Andalusia prone to hyper-turbid events. Fellow 6 tested her GPU-based 2D shallow-water multi-scalar and multi-layer transport model here to assess sediment-transport modeling.

Fellows: Laure Sicard

WP1 · Coastal hazards

Limfjord

Denmark

Sound across northern Jutland that passes through Aalborg. DC1 Gábor Albrecht uses it as a test case for water-level prediction with MIKE 3 Flow Model (DHI), a widely used three-dimensional model of currents and water levels driven by wind, tides and atmospheric pressure. It provides an established reference for wind-driven water levels while he develops the wind–wave coupling of his project (WP1).

Fellows: Gábor Albrecht

WP2 · River & estuary dynamicsWP4 · Water quality

Mondego River estuary – Figueira da Foz

Portugal

Estuary of the Mondego, the largest river entirely within Portugal. Fellows 9 and 5 built a one-way nested 2D–3D (finite-volume / OpenFOAM) model of salinity intrusion, validated with buoy data. Coimbra lab flumes reproduce a stretch of the Mondego from real topography for dye-tracer pollutant experiments. Águas da Figueira is an associated partner.

Fellows: Adhemar Romero, Mengyun Wang

WP1 · Coastal hazardsWP3 · Urban flooding

Senigallia – Misa River estuary

Italy

Low-lying microtidal estuary of the Misa River on the central Adriatic coast, about 35 km northwest of Ancona. Fellow 8 studies compound sea–river flooding and wave-driven urban flooding here. Long-term monitoring (stream gauges, tide gauge, offshore acoustic profiler, video monitoring, X-band radar) supplies data to calibrate and validate the consortium models. The Comune di Senigallia (Civil Protection) is an associated partner.

Fellows: Bekan Chelkeba Tumsa
