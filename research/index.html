Research

Following water from the open coast to the city

Four connected work packages develop the models, methods and data needed to forecast coastal hazards, river and estuary dynamics, urban flooding and water quality.

Waves generated at sea reach the coast, push into estuaries and meet rivers coming the other way. During storms and heavy rain, that water can spill into cities, and it carries sediments, bacteria and plastics with it.

RESCUER studies this chain as a whole. Each of our four research themes covers one link, and the doctoral projects share methods, codes and data across them: wave models feed estuary models, estuary models feed urban flood models, and all of them feed the transport of pollutants.

How we work

Models, computing, experiments and data

Every theme combines the same four ingredients, so methods developed in one project can be reused in another.

  • Numerical models

    Boussinesq-type, shallow-water and coupled multi-physics models, built on stable, accurate numerical schemes.

  • GPU computing

    Codes parallelized on graphics processors, fast enough for large domains and operational forecasting.

  • Laboratory experiments

    Flume and basin tests of waves, urban flows and debris that validate the models against measurements.

  • Field data at study sites

    Real estuaries, coasts and fjords where models meet observations from partners and local authorities.
